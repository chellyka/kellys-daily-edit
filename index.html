01 — Finance & Markets

Anthropic’s listing looms over a wobbly AI trade.

Chips sell off after Nvidia’s CEO exit, yields stay high.

Anthropic’s IPO is set to be the biggest listing ever

An amended S-1 (10 Nov) offers 825M shares at $70–78: about $64.4B at the top, plus a $26B non-voting placement with Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC, for roughly $90.4B against SpaceX’s $85.7B in July. Bloomberg reportedly has it about 6× oversubscribed. Pricing is expected Wed 18 Nov and trading Thu 19 Nov as ANT on Nasdaq, but Anthropic has not publicly confirmed the date. Valuation estimates range from about $1.2T (CNBC Pro) to $1.4T (other reports). Sunday reports add that the FT says a price above the range is being considered, about 20% of shares are reserved for retail, and banks reportedly turned away orders from China and Hong Kong; Japanese brokers take retail applications until 19 Nov (Nikkei). One strategist warned the Nvidia backdrop is “not what Anthropic needed”. The trend: AI mega-listings are now the market’s main source of fresh equity. Disclosure: Anthropic makes Claude.

Il Messaggero (citing FT) · Nikkei · Yahoo Finance · CNBC Pro · Alliance News · Finwire · Benzinga

Nvidia’s sixth straight weekly drop

Jensen Huang resigned Thursday and CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO. Nvidia fell 2.01% to $162.85 Friday and about 13% on the week, its longest losing run since 2012, wiping out more than $570B of value by Eastmoney’s count (CNA says over $600B). The Philadelphia chip index fell 3.43%, and AMD dropped over 8% Friday after a roughly 18% weekly gain. Reports tie the exit to personal-conduct and national-security concerns. Nvidia reports its first results since the exit on Tue 17 Nov (US), and Huang may face questions from Congress that week. The trend: AI money is rotating between chip names, not leaving.

Wall Street still booked a winning week

The S&P 500 closed at 7,561.67 (−0.4%) but gained 2.51% on the week, and the Nasdaq gained 3.53% and the Dow 2.07%; the Nasdaq and S&P both ended four straight weekly declines (Yicai). On Friday the Dow rose 0.1% to 52,347.55 and the Nasdaq fell 0.91% to 25,136.27. Technology was the weakest sector (−1.7%); staples and financials led. The trend: a broad market holding up while AI leaders swing hard.

02 — World & New Zealand

A three-way summit, and a count still running.

Shenzhen hosts Trump, Xi and Putin; NZ waits for special votes.

Trump, Xi and Putin to meet in Shenzhen

The White House confirmed Friday the first-ever three-way meeting of the US, Chinese and Russian leaders, on the sidelines of APEC (18–19 Nov). Trump meets Xi for a bilateral and working lunch on Thursday 19 Nov, with the trilateral at the end of the visit. Nuclear arms reductions and the Iran war are on the agenda; Ukraine is not. A senior US official said, “We do not expect to get to any kind of agreement.” Elon Musk will travel with Trump but not attend the meeting. Earlier reports (Al Jazeera, SCMP) treated the trilateral as unconfirmed and said Beijing looked lukewarm, so watch for last-minute changes. The trend: symbolism over substance: analysts expect few breakthroughs.

Alliance News · AP via MS NOW · Al Jazeera

NZ election: preliminary result, final count 27 Nov

A press release quoting the Electoral Commission puts National, ACT and NZ First at a combined 67 of 131 seats. The Opportunity Party, led by Qiulae Wong, enters Parliament with about 6% and seven seats (1News); Swarbrick says it is “time to pick a side”. About 399,000 special votes are still to be counted, and official results are due Fri 27 Nov. The trend: a thin majority plus a new kingmaker means coalition talks could drag.

Benzinga (press release) · 1News

Iran: Trump says no deal needed, Iran disputes talks

Trump said this week the US no longer needs an agreement with Iran, and the US naval blockade continues. Iran’s security chief reportedly accused Washington of fabricating reports of negotiations. Brent closed near $95.84 Friday, about 9.5% lower on the month. The trend: oil is easing on diplomacy headlines while the standoff is unresolved.

Benzinga · Nasdaq/RTT

NZ economy: housing and spending soft

QV says the national average home value fell 0.9% in the three months to October to NZ$890,508. Card spending slipped 0.7% in October with petrol bills crowding out other goods (Westpac), while the manufacturing PMI eased to 54.9 from 56.3. The trend: a cautious consumer while exporters and tourism hold up.

MT Newswires · NBR

Auckland Airport emergency crews give strike notice

E tū Union served notice that all 69 airport emergency services staff will withdraw labour for two 24-hour periods this month; the dates were not in my source. Separately, Air New Zealand says it will expand its 2027 international schedule with four new aircraft. The trend: tourism demand is firm, helped by a soft kiwi dollar, even as labour disputes loom. Check with your airline before travelling.
